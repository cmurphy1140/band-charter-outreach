# Competitor operations — what student-performance travel operators publish about how they run

[Provider research method](../carnegie-hall/strategies/02-market-and-provider-research.md) · [Offer evidence](../carnegie-hall/BUSINESS-CONTEXT.md) · [Prior MTC source register](../carnegie-hall/research/partner-pilot/SOURCES.md)

Retrieval and review date: **September 17, 2026**, unless a row says otherwise. Every
factual claim below carries a source ID that resolves to an exact URL and access date in
the [source register](#source-register).

## Purpose

This document reads what comparable operators put in public about the machinery they run:
how a trip is quoted, how supplier commitments are recorded, how money and traveller data
are collected, and what happens when a director asks for a change.

It was commissioned against a stated operating problem: **a director asks for a change,
the whole proposal is redrafted by hand, and the vendor calls get made again from memory,
because the printed document is the only record of what suppliers agreed.** The question
is not "who is best" — it is "who has published a way of avoiding that, and what would it
cost a one-person shop to adopt."

**That premise is a brief, not a finding.** It was supplied to this research task; it is
not established anywhere in this repository and no evidence for it was gathered here.
[The offer evidence](../carnegie-hall/BUSINESS-CONTEXT.md) records the adjacent state as
still open — the observed tools are folders, Word and PDF proposals, spreadsheet budgets,
Outlook and DocuSign, and "School approval sequencing, who owns each step, the current
follow-up cadence … still require confirmation." Practices 2 and 5 below are built on the
premise and inherit its status. Confirm the premise with the business before treating
either as a decision. In the same spirit, "Troen is building an operations layer" is the
task framing, not a project commitment: CLAUDE.md records broader travel software as
**conditional future work**.

This is a read of public marketing, FAQ and terms pages. It is not a market sizing, not a
pricing study, and not a recommendation to approach any company named here.

### These are not all competitors

Four of the twelve operators profiled — **PACE Travel, Bob Rogers Travel, Green Light and
Super Holiday Tours** — are named in Troen's own March 31 schedule. [The offer
evidence](../carnegie-hall/BUSINESS-CONTEXT.md) states: "The March 31 schedule names Pace
Travel, Bob Rogers Travel, Green Light Tours/Travel, and Super Holiday Tours. Publicly
research these organizations now, verifying their names and services. Confirm relationship
status and roles before any actual approach. A company can be a partner for one event and
a competing provider in another context."

The file name "competitor operations" is therefore wrong for at least those four, and the
[provider research method](../carnegie-hall/strategies/02-market-and-provider-research.md)
lists "a partner is not mislabeled solely as a competitor" as a quality check. Read every
profile below as **an operations read, not a competitive classification**. Nothing here
establishes that any named company is a rival rather than a partner, or the reverse.

One identity question is also unresolved: the schedule says "Green Light Tours/Travel"
while the site read here (GL-01) trades as **Green Light Group Tours**. This pass did not
verify that these are the same organisation, and strategy 02 requires that verification
before the name is reused.

## Method

Following the comparison rules in
[02 — Market, provider comparison, and channel hypotheses](../carnegie-hall/strategies/02-market-and-provider-research.md):

- **Public pages only.** Company marketing pages, FAQ pages, and terms-of-service pages
  served without a login. No portal was entered, no account was created, and no credential
  was used. Pages were fetched one at a time, a handful per host.
- **Two robots.txt checks, not seventeen.** `bobrogerstravel.com` disallows only WordPress
  admin and form-export paths, and `groupcollect.com` allows all agents (RB-01, RB-02).
  **Those two checks cover two origins.** Fifteen other origins were fetched without a
  robots check, and because `robots.txt` is per-origin the `groupcollect.com` result
  governs neither `www.groupcollect.com` (GC-01) nor the operator subdomains
  `peakgrouptravel.groupcollect.com` (PK-02) and `gogmt.groupcollect.com` (GMT-01). The
  fetches were few, slow, human-scale and to pages built for public reading, so the risk
  is low — but this document does **not** establish robots compliance for those fifteen
  origins, and CLAUDE.md treats respecting robots.txt as non-negotiable. Check the
  remaining origins before any repeat or wider pass.
- **Nothing was cached.** CLAUDE.md requires caching fetched pages under `data/raw/`;
  these pages were read through a fetch tool and no copy was written, so `data/raw/` holds
  nothing for any host in the register. Every quoted sentence and every figure here is
  therefore re-verifiable only by re-fetching the live page, and a later reader cannot
  distinguish a stale quote from a misquote. This is a known gap in this pass, not a
  decision that caching is unnecessary.
- **Stated versus inferred.** A claim marked *stated* is language the company publishes
  about itself. A claim marked *inferred* is my reading of published evidence and is
  labelled as such every time. Marketing adjectives ("seamless", "world-class") are not
  recorded as operational facts.
- **Silence is not absence.** Most of these companies publish a fraction of how they
  operate. Where a field below is empty it means *not published on the pages I read* —
  never *the company does not do this*. Section
  [What this does not establish](#what-this-does-not-establish) restates this.
- **No individuals.** No employee name, personal mailbox or direct phone number is
  recorded. Several pages I read name staff; those names are deliberately omitted.
  Company-level group-sales channels and published emergency lines are referenced by
  description only.
- **No margin reverse-engineering.** Where a fee, deposit or penalty is published it is
  quoted as published. Nothing is inferred about cost, net rate or commission.
- **Standard inclusions are not advantages.** A 24/7 line, a tour director, and an online
  payment page are near-universal in this set. Recording that an operator has one is not
  evidence it is differentiated by it.

Twelve operators are profiled. Two more were read and are recorded in
[Also checked, not profiled](#also-checked-not-profiled) because their published detail is
thin but non-redundant.

### Which comparison this is

Strategy 02 requires two comparisons be kept apart: **Troen versus other
performance-event providers**, which is the customer's choice, and **proposed support
versus existing software and services**, which is Troen's own workflow and spending. This
document deliberately runs the second one and only touches the first incidentally.

That means the [practices](#practices-worth-adopting) — in particular practice 3, on
buying rather than building the money layer — are **workflow-and-spending findings**, and
strategy 02 routes those to
[07 — software and automation fit](../carnegie-hall/strategies/07-software-and-automation-fit.md).
This unit owns only this file and did not update that strategy or `PROGRESS.md`; the
coordinator should carry the software conclusions across and register this document, or
they will not be found from the strategy index.

## Operator profiles

### Music Travel Consultants

MTC is the operator this repository has already researched in depth; the 2026-09-11
register in [the partner pilot sources](../carnegie-hall/research/partner-pilot/SOURCES.md)
holds its founding (1987, Indianapolis), its divisions, its affiliations, and its
published "acts only as Agent" responsibility statement. This pass adds one thing, and it
is the single most useful operational disclosure in the whole set.

MTC's **Trip Account** is described as "an internet-based program that provides … a
complete trip resource center 24 hours a day", with secure document access and financial
tracking, plus an iOS/Android app carrying interactive checklists, pop-up and emergency
communications, daily itineraries, group messaging and a photo gallery (MTC-A). Payment
takes "fewer than five minutes" and accepts four card brands and electronic checks
(MTC-A).

The operationally important sentence is on the director side: leaders "assign travelers to
the appropriate rooms, coaches, flights or chaperones" by clicking, which MTC presents as
eliminating manual typing of "rooming lists, coach lists, air manifests or chaperone
assignments" (MTC-A).

*Inference (mine):* that is only possible if MTC holds one traveller record per person and
renders the rooming list, coach list, air manifest and chaperone sheet as **views** over
it, rather than as four hand-maintained documents. MTC does not describe its data model,
and I have not seen the system. But a click-to-assign interface that emits four different
list types is not a document workflow.

### Bob Rogers Travel

BRT is the explicit positioning case this research was asked to examine. Its educational
page says: "Other companies often offer 'travel by template' or the same tour for every
group. That's never been our style", followed by "We customize each and every travel
experience to the group we're working with, listening to every detail and reflecting them
in your personalized itinerary" (BRT-03). The performance page repeats it as "there's no
such thing as an 'off the shelf' tour" (BRT-02).

That claim is about the **client-facing itinerary**. What BRT publishes about its own
machinery reads differently. Its technology page names exactly two products (BRT-04):
**BRT Payments**, where "each traveler has their own account", "both director and
individual student families can login", with automatic payment reminders, no late fees,
director-level payment status review, and fundraising money applied to student accounts;
and the **BRT Moments that Matter app**, for private photo sharing, group communication,
location tracking and document storing. The payments page adds the operational promise:
"You simply login to the system and quickly see which students have paid and which ones
haven't", and "Directors never have to touch the money or 'hunt down' students who are
late to pay, that's our job" (BRT-05).

The parents-and-students page links "Log In to BRT Payments" to
`bobrogerstravel.groupcollect.com` (BRT-07). *Inference (mine):* BRT Payments is a
white-labelled deployment of the third-party GroupCollect platform (GC-01), not in-house
software. The hostname is strong evidence of hosting; it is **not** evidence about who
owns the data, what the contract says, or whether BRT runs other systems alongside it.

Scale is published: over 10,000 trips, 750,000 students, all 50 states and over 30
countries, since the fall of 1981 (BRT-01, BRT-02). Cancellation is published in useful
shape — group cancellation 91+ days out returns 100% "less any non-refundable vendor
payments", 90 days or fewer returns the remainder less a 15% service charge, and "Refunds
are not available for individual cancellations", which is what travel protection is sold
against (BRT-06).

*Reading worth keeping:* BRT's bespoke claim and its templated machinery are not in
conflict. The itinerary is bespoke; the ledger, the reminders and the photo app are
standard product. That is the shape a small operator should copy — differentiate where the
customer looks, standardise everywhere else.

### WorldStrides

The largest operator in the set, and the one whose published operations are most about
**owned presence** rather than software. It states "Staffed offices in over 40 countries"
and "1,600+ Field educators trained to lead our programs every year" (WS-01, WS-02).
Health and safety is published as named infrastructure: a **WorldAssist** emergency
response command centre "staffed 24/7 by our specially trained team members", a partnership
with an international private security company, 24/7 access to medical care through a
university medical faculty practice, and telehealth access to a mental health professional
through a behavioural-health provider (WS-03). Company emergency lines are published on
that page; they are not reproduced here.

Traveller-facing, the portal is comparatively plain: create an account, enter the program
or trip ID supplied by the program leader, have a payment method ready (WS-04). Scale is
"Since 1964, over 10 million students" and "10,000+ Teacher-led programs brought to life
each year" (WS-01).

*Inference (mine):* at 10,000+ programs a year, the ground-operations answer is employed
staff in destination rather than a per-trip supplier scramble. Nothing on the pages I read
describes how suppliers are sourced or contracted, so this is an inference from scale and
staffing claims only.

### Peak Performance Tours / Peak Group Travel

Peak publishes the crispest **operational calendar** in the set, and it is published as
terms rather than as guidance (PK-02):

- rooming list due **60 days** before departure;
- all accounts, group-pay or individual-pay, paid in full **45 days** before departure, and
  "Passengers will not be permitted to participate in the Tour if their account is not paid
  in full";
- latest air-ticket name change **30 days** before departure;
- $15 per passenger for each late installment;
- cancellation at >90 days, 90–30 days (15% admin fee plus non-refundable vendor costs),
  and <30 days (100% non-refundable);
- insurance "available and recommended, but not required";
- and the agency statement: "PEAK does not own or operate any entity which is to or does
  provide goods or services for your trip", alongside a right to "substitute hotels or
  attractions of a similar category".

The payment page itself is a thin shell that hands off to
`peakgrouptravel.groupcollect.com` (PK-01), and the terms are served from that same
GroupCollect host. Note the two trading names — Peak Performance Tours and Peak Group
Travel — appearing on the same property (PK-01).

### Kaleidoscope Adventures

Established 1993 (KA-01). Two published details matter.

First, on-trip coverage is stated as a role, not an adjective: "a 24/7 Professional Tour
Director assigned to your group", giving "constant communication, coordination, and
troubleshooting", plus a staffed 24-hour emergency line during the trip (KA-01).

Second, the money layer is openly a third-party platform: the trip account login resolves
to `mykatrip.wetravel.com` (KA-02). Its distinctive feature is fundraising built into the
traveller record — "a unique link for EACH student to request help funding his or her
travel", with donors paying "DIRECTLY to the student's individual account", and the student
choosing who receives the link (KA-02).

Cancellation is a three-step ladder ending at 100% non-refundable inside 35 days, with
optional cancel-for-any-reason protection that is excluded in New York (KA-01).

That exclusion is **not a new finding**. [The offer
evidence](../carnegie-hall/BUSINESS-CONTEXT.md) already records it from Troen's own
supplied protection sheet (item I02): an Enhanced plan with "a stated 75% Cancel for Any
Reason benefit with eligibility/timing conditions, including a New York restriction."
Kaleidoscope's page independently corroborates that the restriction is a market-wide
feature of these products rather than one vendor's quirk — which matters for a March 3 New
York event, and matters more for the wording than for the choice of supplier.

### Grueninger Music Tours

GMT publishes the most explicit **change-pricing** in the set, which is exactly the
mechanism the briefed problem describes as missing — see the premise caveat in [Purpose](#purpose) (GMT-01):

- "Any addition, subtraction or name change from the original passenger list must be made
  in writing";
- "Name changes are accepted without penalty up to 45 days before departure … From 45 to 14
  days before departure, there will be a name change charge assessed, currently $50/person
  … No name changes are accepted within 14 days of departure";
- additions inside 45 days incur $50;
- "Late payments/deposits and required forms are charged a daily service fee of $15.00 per
  occurrence, per late day. For a maximum late charge of $210.00 per occurrence".

It also disclaims a job many operators quietly absorb: "Grueninger is not responsible for
rooming decisions and will not be liable for any disputes, claims, injuries" (GMT-01). The
contracting entity is published as Grueninger Cruises and Tours, Inc., and the terms are
served from `gogmt.groupcollect.com`.

### Encore Tours

A division of ACIS and AIFS (EN-01), which is worth noting because its traveller experience
looks like a large-parent product rather than a boutique one.

**My Account** is "a password protected site that includes all the details for your
upcoming trip", where a traveller can access the day-by-day itinerary, read notes from the
group leader, make online payments, enrol in automatic payments, and update their own
contact and **passport** information (EN-02). That last item is the quiet one: traveller
data is maintained by the traveller, not collected by the director and retyped.

Encore publishes a two-track payment design — an automatic plan described as more flexible
with no strict deadlines, and a manual plan with dated milestones, a $200 or $495 initial
payment depending on registration date, and a $50 deposit late fee with possible
cancellation for missing the $495 deadline (EN-01). Insurance is bundled: "When you travel
with Encore, your insurance is already included, granting you automatic health and trip
cancellation/interruption insurance", upgradeable to 130 days out (EN-01). Cancellations
must come through a form: "We cannot take cancellations over the phone or chat" (EN-01).

Its public FAQ now 301-redirects to a third-party help host (EN-03). *Inference (mine):*
Encore has moved self-service support onto a hosted knowledge-base product. I did not
follow the redirect and take no claim from it.

### Brightspark Travel

"For over 50 years" and "tens of thousands of student groups over the last five decades"
(BS-01). Positioning is squarely custom: "Our custom tours are designed by you, for you.
From STEM tours to band trips, if you can dream it, we can create it!" (BS-01).

Operationally the site is reticent. A portal exists at `portal.brightsparktravel.com` for
registration and payment (BS-01). Safety is asserted in general terms — "industry leaders
in safety and security standards, conducting strict audits and providing 24/7 support" —
with one concrete commercial term, **Refund Guarantee Protection**, stated to return "every
penny spent on trip payments when a tour is cancelled" on domestic tours (BS-01). The
traveller resources page carries downloads and navigation but no operational detail
(BS-02).

The audits are asserted without a published standard, auditor or scope. That is a marketing
claim, not an operational disclosure, and it is recorded as such.

### Music Celebrations International

MCI's published operations are almost entirely its **Web Portal** at
`portal.musiccelebrations.com`, described as "an online system with capabilities to link
information, view documents for your tour, and stay connected with your group director and
team at Music Celebrations International (MCI)" (MCI-01).

Two details are unusual for this set. MCI publishes a **security posture** — TLS 1.2 on
every interaction and on uploads, and card and bank details used to process the payment and
then deleted rather than stored (MCI-01). And it accepts **passport uploads** through the
portal (MCI-01), which means the portal is a traveller-document system, not only a payment
page.

*Inference (mine):* publishing the encryption version and the no-card-storage rule reads as
a response to school and district procurement questions. A small operator collecting
passports and dates of birth will be asked the same questions, and having a one-paragraph
answer ready is cheap.

### Super Holiday Tours

"Creating unique travel experiences since 1975" (SH-01). Its FAQ is the most candid in the
set about the limits of customisation: "Your itinerary will be customized for your group.
That being said, everything must be done as a group whole" (SH-01). That is the real
constraint behind every bespoke claim in this document — the itinerary flexes, the group
moves as one unit, and that is what makes suppliers bookable at all.

Other published specifics: no credit card fees on the online account and no late fees
(SH-01); "A Tour Director is offered to every group" (SH-01); the protection-plan decision
must be made within 14 days of the initial deposit (SH-01); and a four-step cancellation
ladder — 90–61 days 25%, 60–45 days 50%, 44–30 days 75%, 29–0 days 100% — with deposits,
the protection plan and convenience fees non-refundable at any time (SH-01).

Rooming ownership is stated plainly and pushed to the customer: "The rooming list is created
by the group leader" (SH-01).

### Green Light Group Tours

Founded 2003 (GL-01). The most interesting published item for a small operator is the
**trip leader dashboard**: alongside traveller accounts that pay by card, group leaders get
a view to track payments and manage logistics (GL-01). That is the same two-sided shape as
BRT Payments and MTC Trip Account, at a much smaller company.

Green Light also publishes its scope boundary rather than implying unlimited capacity: "We
plan full-service overnight trips for groups of 30 or more travelers and day trips for 100
or more travelers" (GL-01), and states it coordinates "transportation, lodging, attractions,
meals, security, and support so group leaders can focus on students" (GL-01). Guides and
group managers travel with trips. No cancellation terms, insurance statement or deadline
schedule is published on the page read.

### PACE Travel

The thinnest published operations in the set, and included because it is a named
comparator. Planning is described as consultative — "Walk us through your goals and
expectations and we'll build a plan catered uniquely to you and your students" (PC-01) —
and the payment and new-trip registration routes both resolve to
`pacetravel.groupcollect.com` (PC-01). Scale is "decades of experience" with no figure.

No terms, deadlines, insurance, chaperone or on-trip staffing detail was published on the
page read. Under this document's rules that is a gap in my reading, not a gap in PACE.

## Also checked, not profiled

**Educational Travel Adventures (ETA)** publishes the only explicit chaperone ratio I found:
"ETAs chaperone to student ratio is typically 1:10, but can be changed based upon your
request" (ETA-01). It also publishes an ETA Tour Manager on each bus, a per-school **Trip
Portal** described as "An all-in-one planning, sign-up, and communication website for your

policy (ETA-01). On change handling it states that before departure "our team works closely
with educators to manage updates and provide clear guidance on available options", and on

details", and signing it is what triggers "ETI to begin booking all your group tour

*Inference (mine):* ETI's Travel Agreement is the same "the printed document is the record"
pattern the brief attributes to Troen, just formalised and countersigned. That it works at ETI's size
suggests the pattern is not fatal — but ETI also reserves unilateral change rights, which is
how a document-of-record survives change without a redraft.

## The shared machinery underneath

The clearest single finding of this pass is not about any one operator.

Four of the twelve profiled operators serve something from a **GroupCollect** host, and the
distinction between them matters:

- **Bob Rogers Travel** (BRT-07), **Peak** (PK-01) and **PACE** (PC-01) route their
  *registration or payment* entry points there — observed, because the login or "proceed to
  payment" link on the operator's own page goes to that host.
- **Grueninger** (GMT-01) and **Peak** (PK-02) serve their *terms of service* there. That
  their registration also runs on it is an **inference** from the host, not something
  observed.

A fifth operator, **Kaleidoscope**, serves its trip account from a **WeTravel** host
(KA-02), observed the same way.

So, of twelve profiled operators: **four observed** routing registration or payment through
third-party software (Bob Rogers Travel, Peak and PACE on GroupCollect; Kaleidoscope on
WeTravel), and **one inferred** from a terms host alone (Grueninger). That is a **floor,
not a rate** — the remaining seven may run the same software behind a hostname that does
not reveal it, and this method can only see the ones that do. Nothing here supports a claim
about how common the practice is.

This is also not new to the repository. [The offer
evidence](../carnegie-hall/BUSINESS-CONTEXT.md) records GroupCollect on Troen's own
supplied travel-protection sheet (item I01), alongside the note that "the supplied
materials do not prove which payment platform is currently configured" — which is the
actual open question, and one this document cannot answer from outside.

GroupCollect describes itself as "The platform where group travel works together" and offers
to help operators "Build trips, get passengers on board, and manage daily operations", built
for tour operators, suppliers, trip leaders and passengers (GC-01). It names two halves:
**GroupCollect Register**, to "accept registrations and payments, offer trip protection, and
collect the data you need from your passengers" (GC-01); and **GroupCollect Works**, for
supplier communication — "Ask about availability, receive proposals, book and pay" (GC-01).

The supplier half is the part that speaks directly to the briefed problem (GC-02):

- "Send and receive booking inquiries";
- "No more messy email threads when requesting quotes. With GroupCollect Works, the RFP
  process is standardized and simplified";
- "Get proposals, book, and chat through the details with suppliers";
- "Send and receive contracts, forms, rooming lists and any other documents";
- "The booking document lives in the chat as well as your trip view";
- "Centralized view of all your suppliers, trips and trip components";
- "Make and receive payments", including logging payments made outside the system;
- "Easily import trips from other systems using CSVs";
- and the framing complaint it is sold against: operators "jumping between inboxes and
  separate systems".

*Inference (mine), clearly labelled:* the operators above are visibly using the
**registration and payment** half. Nothing I read shows any of them using the supplier half.
Marketing language from a software vendor describes a product; it is not evidence that any
named operator has adopted it. I have not used the product, priced it, or seen a deployment.

What the evidence does support is narrower and still useful: mid-size student-travel
operators in this market **buy** the traveller money-and-data layer rather than building it,
and at least one vendor in that market has already productised the supplier-commitment
record that the brief describes as living only in a printed proposal and in memory.

## Practices worth adopting

Each entry names who publishes it, why it fits a one-person operation, and what adopting it
would actually cost. Cost estimates are my judgement about effort, not quotes.

### 1. Hold one traveller record and generate every list from it

**Published by:** Music Travel Consultants — leaders "assign travelers to the appropriate
rooms, coaches, flights or chaperones" by clicking, instead of typing "rooming lists, coach
lists, air manifests or chaperone assignments" (MTC-A). Encore lets the traveller maintain
their own contact and passport details (EN-02); MCI accepts passport uploads into the portal
(MCI-01).

**Why it fits a small operator:** the rooming list, the coach list, the meal count and the
chaperone sheet are four renderings of the same set of people. Maintained as four documents
they drift, and every change means four edits plus a redraft. Maintained as one list with
assignment fields, a change is one edit and the four outputs regenerate.

**Cost to adopt:** low, and it is the highest-leverage item here. One table of travellers
with `room`, `coach`, `flight` and `chaperone_group` columns, plus four render functions.
This is days of work, not months, and it does not require buying anything.

### 2. Keep the supplier's commitment next to the trip, not only in the proposal

**Published by:** GroupCollect Works — "Send and receive contracts, forms, rooming lists and
any other documents", "The booking document lives in the chat as well as your trip view",
"Centralized view of all your suppliers, trips and trip components", "Keep all your notes in
one place" (GC-02).

**Why it fits a small operator:** this is the exact failure described at the top of this
document. When the printed proposal is the only record, a change forces the vendor calls to
be made again from memory. If each trip carries a **component record** per supplier — who,
what, how many, what price, quoted on what date, held until when, confirmed by which
document — then a director's change becomes a diff against that record rather than a blank
redraft.

**Cost to adopt:** moderate but bounded. A per-trip list of components with supplier, status
(`enquired` / `quoted` / `held` / `confirmed`), quantity, price basis, quote date, hold
expiry, and a link to the document that confirms it. No integration is needed; the value is
in writing the commitment down at the moment it is made.

### 3. Buy the money-and-traveller-data layer instead of building it

**Published by:** the hosting evidence across four profiled operators on GroupCollect
(BRT-07, PK-01, GMT-01, PC-01) and one on WeTravel (KA-02), plus the feature set
GroupCollect Register publishes — registrations, payments, trip protection, "collect the
data you need from your passengers" (GC-01). Read the distinction between observed and
inferred hosts in [the shared machinery](#the-shared-machinery-underneath) before weighing
this.

**Why it fits a small operator:** collecting money from hundreds of families means card
handling, refunds, chargebacks, PCI scope, reminder emails and reconciliation. BRT's own
promise — "Directors never have to touch the money or 'hunt down' students who are late to
pay, that's our job" (BRT-05) — is a support commitment, not a feature, and a solo developer
should not also be a payments company. That argument stands on the nature of the work, not
on anyone else's hosting.

**Cost to adopt: not established here.** No vendor publishes platform pricing on the pages
read, and this document does not guess at a fee structure. The only pricing in the
repository is adjacent, not comparable: [the offer
evidence](../carnegie-hall/BUSINESS-CONTEXT.md) records TravMark/GroupCollect *travel
protection* at a displayed 5% and 7.9% of trip cost (item I01), which is an insurance rate
charged to travellers, not a platform fee charged to the operator. Get a quote before
budgeting.

**Two hedges this recommendation must carry.** First, that BRT Payments runs on
GroupCollect is an inference from a login hostname (BRT-07); it shows where the login is
served and nothing about contracts, data ownership or what else BRT runs. It is suggestive,
not probative, and the recommendation does not depend on it. Second, [the offer
evidence](../carnegie-hall/BUSINESS-CONTEXT.md) notes GroupCollect already appears in
Troen's supplied materials while "the supplied materials do not prove which payment
platform is currently configured" — so the first step is finding out what Troen already
has, not selecting a vendor.

### 4. Publish the operational calendar as dated terms, not as reminder emails

**Published by:** Peak — rooming list 60 days, paid in full 45 days, air-ticket name change
latest 30 days (PK-02). Grueninger — name changes free to 45 days, $50 from 45 to 14 days,
none inside 14, additions inside 45 days $50 (GMT-01). Super Holiday — protection-plan
decision within 14 days of deposit (SH-01). Encore — protection upgrade to 130 days out
(EN-01).

**Why it fits a small operator:** a published deadline converts "can we change this?" from an
open-ended favour into a dated, priced decision the director can make without a phone call.
It also gives the operator a defensible reason to say no late in the cycle — which is
precisely when a redraft is most expensive. Peak's "Passengers will not be permitted to
participate in the Tour if their account is not paid in full" (PK-02) is blunt, but it is
knowable in advance by everyone.

**Cost to adopt:** near zero to write, real to enforce. The work is deciding the dates
honestly against actual supplier deadlines, then holding them.

### 5. Track non-refundable supplier exposure by date, and write cancellation against it

**Published by:** Bob Rogers Travel — group cancellation at 91+ days returns 100% "less any
non-refundable vendor payments", at **90 days or fewer** the remainder less a 15% service
charge, individual cancellations are not refunded, and BRT explicitly warns that
non-refundable vendor payments increase as departure approaches (BRT-06). Note the boundary
wording: 91-and-up against 90-and-below leaves no undefined day, which is the property to
copy — a ladder written as "over 90" against "inside 90" would strand day 90 itself. Peak (PK-02), Super Holiday
(SH-01) and Kaleidoscope (KA-01) publish comparable ladders; Kaleidoscope names the quantity
as "unrecoverable supplier fees" (KA-01).

**Why it fits a small operator:** cancellation terms are only writable if you know, on any
given date, how much of the trip you have already committed. That is the same component
record as practice 2, with a `non_refundable_after` date on each line. It also separates the
two very different events a printed proposal conflates: the group cancelling, and one
traveller cancelling.

**Cost to adopt:** low, once the component record exists. One extra field per component plus
a rollup by date.

### 6. Give each group a trip site, not a trip document

**Published by:** Educational Travel Adventures — the Trip Portal is "An all-in-one planning,
sign-up, and communication website for your student trip" (ETA-02), with the teacher's
rooming list accessed through it (ETA-01). Encore's My Account carries the day-by-day
itinerary plus group-leader notes (EN-02). MCI's portal holds tour documents (MCI-01). Green
Light gives the group leader a dashboard to track payments and logistics (GL-01).

**Why it fits a small operator:** a document is a snapshot that goes stale the moment
anything changes, and every stale copy generates a question. A per-trip page rendered from
the trip record is always current, and it is cheap to host. This repository already
demonstrates the pattern — the Carnegie demo is a static site built from reviewed source
records.

**Cost to adopt:** low. One template rendered per trip, published at a private URL. Start
read-only — itinerary, deadlines, what is included, who to call — before attempting sign-up
or payment, which practice 3 says to buy anyway.

### 7. Publish the on-trip staffing and the ratio you actually run

**Published by:** Educational Travel Adventures — "ETAs chaperone to student ratio is
typically 1:10, but can be changed based upon your request", with a tour manager on each bus
(ETA-01). Kaleidoscope — "a 24/7 Professional Tour Director assigned to your group" plus a
staffed 24-hour emergency line (KA-01). Super Holiday — "A Tour Director is offered to every
group" (SH-01). WorldStrides — a 24/7 staffed command centre and named security, medical and
behavioural-health partners (WS-03).

**Why it fits a small operator:** a school administrator approving a trip needs a number and
a name for the role. Most operators in this set publish the role and omit the ratio; ETA
publishes both, and that is the more answerable page. A small operator can publish a true
ratio more easily than a large one, because it knows exactly who is going.

**Cost to adopt:** near zero, and honest only if the ratio is the one actually staffed. Note
that Kaleidoscope's cancel-for-any-reason cover is excluded in New York (KA-01) — worth
checking before promising anything similar for a New York event.

## Practices to avoid

**Claiming internal uniqueness from a client-facing positioning line.** Bob Rogers Travel
markets "not travel by template" (BRT-03) while running standard product underneath — a
payments platform on a third-party host (BRT-07) and a photo-sharing app (BRT-04). The
bespoke claim is about the itinerary and it is fair. What would not be fair is Troen
asserting that *its machinery* is uniquely bespoke, because the machinery is the part
everyone standardises, and the claim is falsifiable in one look at a login hostname. The
project's [comparison method](../carnegie-hall/strategies/02-market-and-provider-research.md)
already forbids calling a standard industry inclusion a unique advantage; this is the same
rule applied to operations.

**Treating any portal in this set as the record of supplier commitments.** Several of these
portals do have a real director side — MTC's Trip Account generates rooming, coach, air and
chaperone lists (MTC-A), BRT Payments offers "director-level payment status review"
(BRT-04), Green Light gives the group leader a dashboard (GL-01) — so this is not a
one-sided-portal complaint. The point is narrower and sharper: **every one of them faces
inward at travellers, families and money.** Not one is published as a place where a hotel's
held-room count, a motorcoach quote or a venue's confirmation lives. Of everything read in
this pass, only GroupCollect Works, a vendor product, is (GC-02). Building an excellent
traveller portal — even one with a strong director view — and believing the vendor problem
is solved would leave the actual failure untouched.

**Importing a penalty schedule you cannot administer.** Grueninger's $15 per day to a $210
cap and $50 name-change fee (GMT-01) and Peak's $15 per late installment (PK-02) are
enforceable at companies with a billing function and a director relationship broad enough to
absorb the friction. A one-person operation that invoices a band parent $15 for a late form
spends relationship capital it needs. Adopt the **deadlines** (practice 4); think hard before
adopting the **fees**.

**Publishing cumulative lifetime numbers as if they were capacity.** "Over 10,000 trips with
750,000 students" (BRT-02) and "Since 1964, over 10 million students" (WS-01) are career
totals across decades. They say nothing about what either operator can run in March 2027, and
a small operator quoting a comparable figure would be describing a past, not an offer. The
repository's existing rule against equating a filename with a booking is the same caution.

**Asserting audit or safety rigour without a named standard.** Brightspark states it conducts
"strict audits" (BS-01) with no auditor, scope or standard published. It may well do exactly
that. But as a model for Troen's own materials, an unverifiable safety adjective is weaker
than one concrete published fact — ETA's 1:10 (ETA-01) or MCI's no-card-storage rule
(MCI-01) — and a school administrator can check the second kind.

**Reading a competitor's silence as a gap.** PACE publishes almost no operational detail
(PC-01) and Brightspark publishes little (BS-01, BS-02). That is a fact about their websites.
Any Troen material implying these operators lack deadlines, insurance or on-trip staff would
be making a claim this research does not support.

## What this does not establish

- **No one was interviewed.** Every statement here comes from a public web page. No director,
  no operator employee, no supplier and no school was contacted.
- **No private system was seen.** No portal was logged into, no account created, no credential
  used, no product trialled. Where this document describes what a portal does, it repeats what
  the vendor or operator *says* it does. Nothing was tested.
- **No outcome was measured.** Nothing here shows that any of these practices reduces
  redrafting, shortens quoting, prevents errors, or wins business. The practices are
  adopted-by-others, not proven-to-work.
- **Platform identification is hosting evidence only.** That BRT Payments, Peak, Grueninger
  and PACE serve registration or terms from a GroupCollect host, and that Kaleidoscope serves
  from a WeTravel host, is an inference from published hostnames. It does not establish
  contract terms, data ownership, which modules are licensed, what any of it costs, or that
  the arrangement is current beyond the access date. It is also a floor rather than a rate:
  an operator whose hostname reveals nothing may still be running the same software.
- **Terms are a snapshot.** Every day count, fee and percentage was read on 2026-09-17
  (MTC-A's underlying post is dated 2022-09-15). Operators change terms without notice, and
  several of these pages are served from vendor-hosted paths that may be versioned
  independently of the operator's own site. Re-check before quoting any figure externally.
- **Silence is not absence.** Empty cells in the comparison table mean the pages read did not
  publish that item. They are not findings that a capability is missing.
- **No pricing, margin or cost conclusion.** Deposits and fees are recorded as published.
  Nothing was inferred about net rates, commission, supplier cost or profitability, in line
  with the project's research rules.
- **No competitive ranking, and no competitive classification.** This document does not
  establish that any operator is better run than any other, nor that Troen would be
  advantaged or disadvantaged against them. It establishes only what each one chose to
  publish. It specifically does **not** establish that any of these companies is a
  competitor: four of the twelve appear in Troen's own March 31 schedule and may be
  partners, as [These are not all competitors](#these-are-not-all-competitors) sets out.
- **The premise was not verified.** The redraft-and-re-call problem this research was aimed
  at was supplied as a brief. No evidence for it was gathered, and none exists elsewhere in
  this repository. Practices 2 and 5 rest on it.
- **Robots compliance and evidence preservation are incomplete.** Two of seventeen origins
  were checked against `robots.txt`, and no page was cached under `data/raw/`, contrary to
  CLAUDE.md's guardrails. Both gaps are stated in the [Method](#method) and must be closed
  before this pass is repeated or widened.
- **This document is not registered anywhere.** This unit owned only this file, so
  `PROGRESS.md` and
  [07 — software and automation fit](../carnegie-hall/strategies/07-software-and-automation-fit.md)
  were not updated. Until the coordinator does that, nothing in the project's status or
  strategy index points here.
- **Coverage is partial.** Twelve operators profiled and two more checked, against the
  thirty-nine public URLs in the source register, across seventeen origins (two of the URLs
  are `robots.txt` checks, and six more carried no operational content and are registered as
  such rather than silently dropped). Pages that appeared only in search-result summaries
  were not fetched and are not cited. Other organisations in the wider market were not read
  in this pass. Two names that were suggested for this research should **not** be treated as
  unexamined competitors: **Musicians Abroad** is recorded in this repository as a *division
  of Music Travel Consultants*, the first operator profiled above
  ([partner pilot sources](../carnegie-hall/research/partner-pilot/SOURCES.md), claim C13),
  so researching it separately would double-count MTC; and **Gateway Music Festivals**
  appears nowhere else in this repository, so it is a new candidate rather than a known one.
