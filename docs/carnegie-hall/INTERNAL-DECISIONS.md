# Internal decisions: keep the POC moving

[Start here](README.md) · [Plan](PLAN.md) · [Strategies](strategies/README.md)

This is a working reference for the logistics and software developer. It is **not a questionnaire to send Troen**. Begin with the sources already supplied and public research. Show a useful example before asking the business for information that only matters to a later action.

## How to use this register

For each unknown, decide whether it changes what is being demonstrated. If it does not, keep it here. If it changes a claim, simplify the claim or label the example. If it affects an actual commercial action, prepare the concrete material first and then identify the minimum missing fact needed for that action.

For example, an unknown ticket price does not prevent showing the educational experience or a tailored school profile. It does prevent calling an illustrative proposal an approved quote. An unknown relationship owner does not prevent researching a school; it matters before contacting that school.

Avoid scattering the same caveat across every screen. Use a clear overall demonstration label, brief labels where an individual fact could be misunderstood, and detailed evidence here. Do not bury a material uncertainty that would make an offer misleading.

## Timing of the conversation

The MTC pilot is accepted for internal use without resolving the two questions
above. They are a decision register, not instructions to contact the Troens or MTC.
The [reviewed profile](research/partner-pilot/PROFILE.md) supports the small MTC
research candidate now connected to the POC. The
[internal partner sheet](research/partner-pilot/PARTNER-SHEET-DRAFT.md) remains
separate under the user's narrower scope, with no contact recommendation.
No business answers were requested in review.

**Before a demonstration:** the developer checks sources, prepares readable outputs, and marks illustrative behavior. No business questionnaire is required. If an uncertainty makes a claim impossible, omit that claim and demonstrate the surrounding value.

**After interest in a specific deliverable:** ask about the one decision needed to use it. “This draft is ready to adapt to a real quote; the approved festival inclusions and price basis are the remaining inputs” is more useful than requesting a complete business process inventory.

**Before an operating pilot:** establish only the roles, records, permissions, and status rules touched by that pilot. A local weekly brief may need a small opportunity export; it does not require migrating the whole CRM.

**Before expansion:** assess upkeep, recurring benefit, account ownership, and information reuse. Those are future product decisions, not conditions for receiving an impressive first example.

## Recording a resolved decision

Keep the decision, source/date, applicable event, affected deliverables, and responsible business role together. Update the relevant fact once and regenerate dependent material. Preserve the previous value when it explains a change. Record an exception explicitly rather than quietly rewriting a global rule.

This register should shrink as real decisions become relevant. Do not turn every useful future question into an immediate dependency.
