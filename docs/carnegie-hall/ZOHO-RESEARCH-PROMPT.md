### Vendor-policy finding independently checked

On September 10, 2026, the public [Zoho anti-spam policy](https://www.zoho.com/policy.html) required express, provable permission for covered commercial messaging and restricted imports/sends involving third-party or website-collected addresses. The [CRM-specific terms](https://www.zoho.com/crm/terms.html) explicitly apply that policy to commercial email from CRM.

Project consequence: keep public research separate from Zoho marketing or email-enabled imports until the specific permissible handoff is established. A published professional address does not establish permission, and one-to-one CRM email should not be assumed exempt. This records vendor terms and an implementation constraint; it does not settle every legal or account-specific question.

The report's other detailed pricing, support, retention, export, and product claims have not all been independently revalidated in this documentation pass. Treat its use of “verified” as the report's cited public-source status. Recheck applicable terms, current prices, entitlements, and representative exports before acting.

## Copy-ready Deep Research prompt

This preserves the full research scope, revised for the current attribution and demonstration-first priorities. Use the received assessment as additional context for a follow-up; it is not necessary to rerun the whole report before building useful POC examples.

```text
Deeply research Zoho (https://www.zoho.com) for Troen Student Performance Events, a small US business that produces student music festivals and arranges customized group travel.

BUSINESS CONTEXT

Troen works with school music directors and other tour companies. Its workflow includes finding prospects, developing relationships, school approvals, customized proposals, contracts and deposits, registration and payments, hotels and transportation, and performance-event delivery.

Our immediate proof of concept focuses exclusively on Troen’s Carnegie Hall event on March 3, 2027. An employee reports one participating group and approximately ten additional ensemble bookings needed. The exact capacity remains unconfirmed.

Troen reportedly pays “Zoho to find clients.” We do not know which Zoho product it uses, its subscription level, actual spending, implementation partner, or what it receives. Treat this statement as an unverified description. Do not assume Zoho directly supplies leads.

The logistics and software developer is preparing a polished, demonstration-first POC. Do not make the initial recommendations depend on Troen completing an account audit, business questionnaire, or relationship export. Distinguish what can be demonstrated independently from what must be confirmed before a specific live action. There is no established best-performing channel or required order of existing relationships, tour partners, and new schools.

We already have a Python prospect-research pipeline and spreadsheet exports. We want to preserve useful work and make targeted improvements. Do not assume we need a new CRM or a complete software rebuild.

CENTRAL QUESTION

What is Troen likely paying for, what can Zoho actually do to help acquire and convert suitable clients, and what would be the most practical, cost-effective setup for this business?

RESEARCH REQUIREMENTS

1. Explain what Zoho actually provides.
Distinguish:
- Software for managing existing contacts and opportunities.
- Tools that discover new prospects.
- Contact enrichment and third-party data.
- Inbound lead capture and marketing.
- Automation and AI features.
- Services delivered by implementation partners or agencies.

Make clear which capabilities Zoho provides directly and which require additional products, external providers, credits, or human work.

2. Identify relevant products and offerings.
Investigate Zoho CRM, Bigin, Zoho One, Campaigns, Marketing Automation, SalesIQ, and relevant enrichment or AI tools where useful. Explain their differences, overlap, limitations, and suitability for a small school-travel and performance-event business. Avoid an exhaustive catalog of unrelated products.

3. Verify current US prices and actual ownership costs.
Include:
- Plans and editions.
- Monthly versus annual billing.
- Seat minimums and all-employee licensing requirements.
- Contact, email, automation, storage, and API limits.
- Usage credits, add-ons, integrations, and overage charges.
- Onboarding, migration, implementation, training, and support costs.
- Renewal, cancellation, refund, upgrade, and downgrade terms.

Provide illustrative costs for teams of 1, 3, and 5 users. State all assumptions and distinguish published prices, estimates, and quote-only costs. Date the pricing information.

4. Map capabilities to Troen’s workflow.
Evaluate both direct school relationships and partnerships with other tour companies.

For each workflow stage, explain:
- What Zoho could accomplish.
- What information or integrations it needs.
- What remains manual.
- Whether it helps find new business or manage business already identified.
- Whether it offers meaningful near-term value for the March 3 event.

Consider finding appropriate ensembles, verifying relevant adult contacts, tracking conversations, recording approval timelines and group sizes, preparing proposals, and following opportunities through contracts and deposits.

5. Investigate business practices fairly.
Examine billing transparency, renewals, upselling, support quality, partner arrangements, data ownership, exports, account closure, deletion, privacy, AI data use, and switching costs.

Use documented policies and credible independent evidence. Distinguish verified facts, corroborated patterns, individual complaints, allegations, and unknowns. Avoid promotional language and unsupported accusations. Explain any relevant difference between Zoho’s conduct and a reseller’s conduct.

6. Evaluate realistic options.
Compare:
- Keeping and better configuring Troen’s existing Zoho setup.
- Simplifying its subscriptions.
- Complementing Zoho with our existing research pipeline.
- Replacing specific functions only where evidence justifies it.

Separate immediate Carnegie Hall priorities from longer-term operational improvements. Do not recommend migration merely because an alternative exists.

DELIVERABLES

Produce a detailed, readable report containing:
- An executive assessment and direct answer to the central question.
- A product/capability comparison.
- A dated pricing and total-cost table.
- A workflow map showing useful capabilities, dependencies, and gaps.
- A balanced assessment of business practices.
- Prioritized recommendations with confidence levels and tradeoffs.
- A selective internal decision register: account information needed for a specific later action, why it matters then, and what we can demonstrate without it. Do not present this as an upfront business homework checklist.
- A concise handoff summary I can paste into the main project task.

SOURCE STANDARDS

Prioritize official pricing pages, documentation, terms, privacy policies, and support material. Supplement with credible independent research where appropriate. Put direct source links beside factual claims, state access dates, and flag regional or edition differences.

Clearly separate verified facts, inference, and missing information. If public evidence cannot establish something, say so. Continue useful research without requiring private account access.

This is research only: do not purchase, sign up, contact vendors, or change systems. Do not request passwords, student information, or confidential signed contracts.
```
