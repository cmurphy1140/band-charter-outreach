# 02 — Market, provider comparison, and channel hypotheses

[Strategy index](README.md) · [Offer evidence](../BUSINESS-CONTEXT.md) · [Zoho assessment](../ZOHO-RESEARCH-PROMPT.md)

## Purpose and output

Build a fair, useful explanation of why a director or tour partner might consider Troen. The output is a focused provider comparison, several short evidence-backed observations about the market, and distinct hypotheses for school and partner channels. It should sharpen the materials and prospect research, not become an exhaustive market report.

Keep two comparisons separate: **Troen versus other performance-event providers** concerns the customer's event/travel choice; **our proposed support versus existing software/services** concerns Troen's workflow and spending. Zoho is not an event producer merely because the team reportedly pays for something described as finding clients.

## Research scope we can execute independently

Begin with a proposed three to five relevant provider offers. Choose for comparability rather than brand recognition alone: similar student ensemble audience, meaningful performance/educational component, and an experience that a director might realistically compare. A provider can also be a partner; record that possibility rather than assigning every company to one permanent category.

Use the partner names visible in the March 31 schedule as research starting points. Publicly verify their current organization names and services. Their appearance in that schedule is not proof of active March 3 commercial terms or a right to approach their schools.

The map guides the initial regional examples but does not define the entire market. Include a relevant exception if evidence supports it. Do not estimate a total addressable market by counting every school in a broad map.

## Step-by-step method

1. **Write the comparison question.** For example: which parts of the performance experience, preparation, and coordination can we document, and which are actually different between comparable offers?
2. **Select the comparison unit.** Compare festival-only to festival-only or a defined trip package to a similar package. Record event year, venue, duration, nights, transport basis, and audience. Flag a partial comparator rather than presenting unlike products as direct substitutes.
3. **Gather primary sources.** Read current event pages, brochures, published eligibility, sample itineraries, inclusions, and pricing terms where public. Record access date and the date/year of the offered experience. Search results are leads to inspect, not final evidence.
4. **Extract a consistent set of fields.** Performance venue/session, preparation/clinic, adjudication options, recording, backstage/director support, eligibility, group requirements, travel scope, commercial conditions, published price basis, and relevant source links.
5. **Normalize only what is justified.** A per-person number excluding transportation cannot be compared directly with a complete trip price. When taxes, occupancy, nights, or complimentary places are missing, say “not directly comparable.” Do not reverse-engineer unpublished margins.
6. **Write the implications.** Separate documented difference, common inclusion, potential strength, and unknown. “Troen documents an assigned event director” can be true even if competitors offer similar support. Uniqueness requires comparative evidence.
7. **Translate findings into useful material.** Choose the two or three observations that change a director sheet, partner explanation, or research criterion. Keep detailed citations in an appendix or expandable notes.

## Output layout

The working matrix should place the providers in parallel columns or rows with a visible “unknown/not published” value. Add short comparison notes when packages differ. A concise narrative should explain what a customer is really weighing: educational value, suitable performance experience, trip burden, approval feasibility, and coordination responsibilities.

Create an internal evidence note for each potential Troen strength: claim, Troen source, comparator source, confidence, and permitted wording. This becomes a useful review layer for persuasive writing. A competitor's silence is not evidence that it lacks a service.

## Channels: testable hypotheses, no fixed ranking

| Channel | Why it might matter | What public research can show | What it cannot establish |
|---|---|---|---|
| Existing relationship clues | Prior context may make a conversation easier | Names and dated evidence already present in supplied material | Current interest, satisfaction, authority, availability, or permission to contact |
| Tour partners | One relationship may connect Troen with several suitable ensembles | School-music specialization, geography, offer types, public business contacts | Access to those schools, net rates, commission, exclusivity, or conversion advantage |
| New schools | Relevant programs may be discoverable beyond existing channels | Ensemble activity, current adult roles, public travel/performance signals | Budget, willingness, approval, or current supplier relationship |

Do not rank these channels by assumed conversion. A POC can show credible examples from all three, and later operational evidence can guide effort. Research accessibility is a valid reason to start somewhere; it is not proof of channel performance.

## Tools and verification

Use Deep Research for a bounded, source-intensive comparison when execution is requested; its report should return usable citations and unresolved questions, not autonomous vendor contact. Use web browsing for claim verification, product-management competitive analysis for structure, and sales/marketing skills for clear audience implications. See the [tools map](../TOOLS-AND-CONNECTORS.md).

Quality checks: all factual provider claims have direct dated sources; price bases align or are visibly incomparable; event dates are current for the claim; a partner is not mislabeled solely as a competitor; and no Troen advantage exceeds the evidence. Trace every statement selected for external material to a checked claim.

## Handoff and stopping point

Give the comparison and wording notes to [materials](05-director-and-partner-materials.md). Give useful evidence-based selection criteria to [prospect research](03-prospect-discovery-and-verification.md). Give software findings to [automation fit](07-software-and-automation-fit.md), preserving the separation between the two comparisons.

Stop when the selected offers support a useful, fair explanation. Troen does not need to supply customer interviews, invoices, or a channel history for this public comparison to be demonstrated. Those inputs become useful only when making account-specific or conversion claims.
