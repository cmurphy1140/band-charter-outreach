# 05 — Director and tour-partner materials

## Purpose and output set

Translate the research into documents that a business person would actually use. The proposed set is a director event sheet, a short director FAQ, a partner sheet, and an editable proposal example. The same source-backed event facts should support all four, with different emphasis for the audience.

These are demonstration materials first. They can be polished and complete as examples without containing an invented price, approved availability, or made-up partner agreement. Use **logistics and software developer** for the project role; the [CS-background statement](../POSITIONING-AND-OPPORTUNITY.md) explains the contribution without employment positioning.

## Audience-specific jobs

| Audience | Document should help them understand | Evidence to use | Avoid assuming |
|---|---|---|---|
| Music director | The performance/educational experience, preparation, likely trip burden, and a useful next conversation | Shared brochure, event-specific date/session evidence, relevant program research | Available budget, school approval, ensemble eligibility, or an accepted invitation |
| School administrator/approver | The educational purpose, basic planning requirements, and which costs/arrangements need a decision | Documented experience plus a clearly proposed approval-summary structure | A district's policy, deadlines, insurance acceptance, or approval status |
| Tour-company partner | What event component might fit an existing trip and how responsibilities could be divided | Public partner services, Troen event evidence, schedule clues | Commission, net rates, exclusivity, access to schools, or a current commercial agreement |

The administrator summary can be a section in the proposal example rather than a separate deliverable. Keep the output set focused.

## Content strategy

**Director sheet:** lead with the event date and the student experience. Explain performance, preparation, and support in a clear sequence. Select only the inclusions supported by the source and accurately qualify shared-brochure details. Use a specific draft next step such as discussing ensemble fit and trip needs. The demo does not need a live contact form or an invented deadline.

**FAQ:** answer the useful questions that evidence can support, including what the experience involves, preparation, and the distinction between festival participation and complete travel arrangements. Where a commercial fact is unknown, say that it is confirmed when preparing a group-specific offer, or omit the unresolved item if it does not help the demo. Keep detailed internal conflicts in the decision register.

**Partner sheet:** explain a possible festival/travel division of responsibility with explicit “proposed” wording. Show the coordination information a partner would need and the benefits that the sources support. Avoid a fake ready-to-sign partner program, invented margins, or a claim that the partner's schools are available to approach.

**Proposal example:** use clearly synthetic group details, with sections for ensemble needs, event experience, travel options, inclusions/exclusions, approval steps, price basis, and next actions. Leave unapproved amounts unquoted. Show how an itinerary change or missing supplier confirmation affects the draft. It should demonstrate structure and care, not impersonate an actual proposal issued by Troen.

## Production method

1. **Create the fact spine.** Pull date, event description, and selected inclusions from the event model. Assign each factual paragraph a claim/source reference internally.
2. **Choose one research case for relevance.** Use a verified program or partner profile to explain why the material has this emphasis. Do not insert an assumed personal concern or fabricate a prior conversation.
3. **Draft content before decoration.** Use plain language, concrete benefits, and readable next steps. Remove model/vendor jargon from the customer material unless it helps a meaningful decision.
4. **Make the source editable.** Prefer DOCX for prose documents and maintain a consistent template. Use PDF only as a checked delivery copy. A spreadsheet or local interface should not be the only place the text can be edited.
5. **Build a coherent visual family.** Reuse typography, spacing, table styles, version labels, and source-note conventions. Use approved authentic assets and avoid implying Carnegie endorsement. Do not copy signatures, personal identifiers, or internal cost tables into a brochure.
6. **Review content and rendering together.** Open the editable file, inspect the rendered pages, check page breaks, table wrapping, footnotes, links, and final page count. Correct content changes in the source, then regenerate the delivery copy.
7. **Connect it to the demonstration.** Link the relevant profile to the correct audience document and an illustrative next-action scenario. Ensure exported filenames and visible titles make the audience and example status clear.

## Persuasion standard

The work should show insight through specificity: a relevant ensemble type, a real dated research signal, a clear educational experience, and awareness that approvals and supplier arrangements determine whether a trip happens. Do not use inflated “AI-powered growth” claims or imply that software guarantees a booking.

Competitive wording must reflect [checked provider evidence](02-market-and-provider-research.md). A useful inclusion can be worth explaining without calling it unique. Productivity figures belong in the internal/business-case discussion as hypotheses, not as a promised result on a school-facing event sheet.

## Tools, checks, and completion

Use document/DOCX/PDF tools for editable sources and rendered verification, design/UX-writing for readability, and sales/marketing skills for audience adaptation. Use the full report sources when needed; do not treat a generated summary as the authority for a commercial promise.

Check March 3 throughout; match all event facts across the four materials; remove any live inventory implication; verify source links; and keep example organizations and activities identifiable as illustrative. Check that the files open independently of the demo.

Completion means a coherent, editable set that makes the value tangible. Before issuing a firm real offer, the applicable price, inclusions, availability, deadline, protection wording, and recipient-specific terms must be confirmed. That later confirmation is not a prerequisite for producing these examples.
