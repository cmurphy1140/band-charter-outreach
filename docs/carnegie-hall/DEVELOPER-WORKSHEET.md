# When I get ahead of myself

[Back to the learning guide](START-HERE-LEARNING.md)

Getting excited and following several ideas at once is understandable. I enjoy
seeing what is possible. I can keep that curiosity without making every possibility
part of today's work.

Seeing more things I don't know does not erase what I already understand. I do not
need to understand the whole system before I can make one useful change.

**An important reminder: I learn by being curious.** I enjoy talking through a
problem with someone who asks thoughtful questions and leaves me room to discover
the answer. I do not need every interest turned into a list of things I should
read, know, or learn. A small hint can help me keep ownership of the discovery;
when I want a direct explanation, I can ask for one.

When the project starts feeling bigger than the thing I sat down to do:

1. **Return to the starting point.** What was I trying to do before this grew?
2. **Separate the interesting idea from the next step.** I can jot the idea down
   without promising to build it.
3. **Make the next step smaller.** Inspect one behavior, read one function, sketch
   one option, or try one reversible change. Choose whichever helps it click.
4. **Give that step an ending.** What would let me say, “That's enough for now”?

If I need a note, three lines are enough:

- What I came here to do: …
- The next small thing I choose: …
- An idea I can come back to: …

I can ask for a hint, a second opinion, or an explanation without handing over the
whole problem. I can also work it out myself. Neither choice needs defending.

**Something I can say to Codex:** “I'm getting ahead of myself. Help me get back to
what I was trying to do. Give me one question or a small hint, and let me think.”

Backing up is part of working through a problem. My excitement can stay; the task
can get smaller. This reminder is here when useful, not another checklist to finish.
