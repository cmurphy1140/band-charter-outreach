## 1. What Troen appears to sell

The materials support two connected roles: producing student performance events and arranging customized group travel. Troen's Carnegie offer includes educational preparation, a performance experience, recognition, and event support. The Orlando proposal shows how a trip can combine performance, transport, lodging, attractions, meals, and chaperone arrangements.

Some groups on the March 31 schedule are associated with outside tour companies. That is evidence of a partner channel worth understanding. A plausible arrangement is that another company supplies travel while Troen supplies the festival; exact responsibilities, commercial terms, and current relationships still need confirmation.

For a music director, the likely decision questions include the educational value, suitability for the ensemble, total trip burden, approval process, and student experience. For a tour partner, relevant questions may include event inclusions, schedule reliability, commercial terms, relationship ownership, and the ease of coordinating an existing trip. These are research hypotheses, not completed customer interviews.

## 4. What the PDFs add

### Carnegie experience — P01

Page 2 lists a matinee or evening performance, group plaque and rating or optional comments-only treatment, archival recording, adjudicator feedback, an event shirt, a one-hour clinic at Boulevard Carroll Music Studios, soundcheck, backstage credentials, an ensemble base room, director dressing-room arrangements, playbills, a director gift, and an assigned event director.

Pages 3–6 describe the sequence: clinic/rehearsal, soundcheck, concert, and awards. They also describe arrival instructions, clothing, movement between spaces, and backstage support. These details help explain the offer's educational and service components. They do not establish that every inclusion is guaranteed for a newly sold March 3 package until Troen confirms it.

The brochure says Troen has produced the concert for over 15 years. Treat that as a brochure claim until corroborated if it is used in competitive positioning. Describe the event as Troen's performance experience at Carnegie Hall; do not infer Carnegie endorsement or that a program staff member is employed by the venue.

### Budget and package construction — P02

Pages 1–3 use a target of 250 paid performers, a stated festival-only figure of $658.12, per-person cost of $373.12, and $285 required profit per person. The fixed-cost total is $85,362.49. These are figures from a working sheet, not achieved margin, a validated break-even calculation, or an approved customer quote.

The venue line specifies an afternoon fee of $28,400 and leaves the evening line blank. That does not match the session and $26,460 fee photographed for March 3. Pages 5–8 include Woodlands and Huntingdon package examples with different festival components, nights, occupancy, and complimentary-place allowances. Some profit and occupancy entries appear unfinished or anomalous. Page 11 is explicitly a 2025 budget with a different performer target.

The usable business insight is that fixed event commitments and per-person travel/package costs interact. Ensemble count, paying performers, complimentary participants, and occupancy affect economics differently. The native workbook, correct event version, actual commitments, and approved rates are needed before using any of these figures to price or forecast March 3. The PDF does not expose formulas for validation.

### Hotel and intermediary relationships — P03

Pages 1–2 identify the March 29–April 1 window and a block of 50 rooms for each of three nights, totaling 150 room nights, with a stated $269 base room rate. Taxes and other conditions are separate. A listed commission is payable to BookMyGroup; do not count it automatically as Troen revenue.

Pages 3–4 describe rooming-list, attrition, payment, cancellation, and other conditions. The document names January 26 and February 26, 2027 payment milestones and a February 26 rooming/cutoff date. These belong to the later event's agreement. Page 6 shows Troen's signature; the supplied copy is not proof of every party's current acceptance, hotel availability, or payment.

This source is useful for understanding the chain from group demand to room blocks, supplier terms, and payment obligations. It does not confirm a March 3 hotel arrangement.

### Customized trip delivery — P04

The proposal combines transportation, three hotel nights, Universal attractions, OrlandoFest performance/adjudication and a clinic, meal arrangements, complimentary packages, discounted chaperone places, an online payment system, and trip support. The price-per-person area is blank in the supplied copy. It cannot establish a final trip price or a paid booking.

The proposed evening departure connects directly to the supplier objection in I11. That pair of sources demonstrates why a draft itinerary must remain distinguishable from a supplier-confirmed arrangement. The cancellation-protection wording also differs from I02 on the cancellation timing: the proposal says 72 hours while the photographed product sheet says two days for its stated benefit. Obtain the applicable approved policy wording before reusing it.

## 6. Competitive questions this evidence supports

Compare relevant providers on the actual offer: performance venue and session, educational preparation, adjudication options, recording, support, group-size/eligibility requirements, travel inclusions, booking conditions, and comparable prices. Keep festival-only and complete-trip offers distinct; taxes, nights, occupancy, transportation, and complimentary places can make headline prices incomparable.

Test three potential strengths: the quality of the performance experience, the work Troen removes from a director's day, and flexibility in fitting the event into a school's or partner's trip. These are hypotheses. Do not call a standard industry inclusion a unique advantage without evidence from a like-for-like comparison.

The March 31 schedule names Pace Travel, Bob Rogers Travel, Green Light Tours/Travel, and Super Holiday Tours. Publicly research these organizations now, verifying their names and services. Confirm relationship status and roles before any actual approach. A company can be a partner for one event and a competing provider in another context.

For directors, learn which approval, cost, or preparation concern determines the decision. For partners, learn who controls the school relationship, what service Troen supplies, and who takes responsibility for the customer and payment. Geography should guide research effort without excluding known exceptions.
