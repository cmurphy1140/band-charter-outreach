## 2. The business case

Help Troen identify suitable ensembles and travel partners, prepare for better conversations, and keep opportunities moving toward bookings—with less repeated research and paperwork.

The immediate focus is March 3, 2027. An employee reports one participating group, and **ten additional ensembles is a provisional target requiring capacity confirmation**. Present the project as a practical way to improve the work around that objective, not as a promise to fill every place.

The first demonstration should make the value visible: a sourced event brief, a relevant prospect with evidence, clear director/partner materials, and an example action list. The logistics and software developer prepares these from the existing evidence and public research; Troen does not need to complete a questionnaire or supply account access first. A polished local presentation can connect the five deliverables, with real research and illustrative activity clearly distinguished. The useful questions are “Does this help us choose whom to approach?”, “Does it improve the conversation?”, and “Does it reduce repeated work or missed handoffs?”

The existing project already supplies research tooling and accessible formats. Its greatest reusable asset is supported information about organizations. The [audit](../AUDIT-2026-09-10.md) shows why accuracy and maintainability need to be demonstrated before making strong claims about the existing list.

## 4. Practical design opportunities

| Item | Design should accomplish |
|---|---|
| Event brief and event sheet | Make the date, experience, inclusions, requirements, and next step easy to find. |
| Itinerary and proposal | Separate confirmed arrangements from proposed options; show readable timing and consistent package facts. |
| Director FAQ | Answer likely approval, preparation, cost, and logistics questions using approved information. |
| Partner sheet | Explain the service being supplied, coordination expectations, and the next commercial conversation without inventing rates or terms. |
| Opportunity workbook | Let the team see why an account matters, what is missing, and who does what next. |
| Compact local demonstration | Let the viewer inspect the event, a sourced opportunity, its tailored materials, and an illustrative next step. |
| Public inquiry page, later | Present a confirmed offer and capture useful inquiries only when live use is selected. |

Keep editable sources and use existing approved brand assets. Favor legibility, consistency, and specific actions over decorative additions. Show a rough, inspectable draft to the logistics and software developer, refine the connected example, and bring the Troens concrete work to react to. Internal costs and contract details belong in internal references, not automatically in customer-facing materials.

The design direction here is a recommendation, not completed visual work. The first materials can stand on their own; a small local presentation can improve inspection without turning the POC into a complete dashboard or website. The [experience strategy](strategies/04-poc-experience-and-design.md) defines the proposed approach.

## 5. A possible connected workflow

These are potential operating increments, not deployed automations or prerequisites for the demonstration. The final column describes facts needed for real use; the POC can use sourced material and explicit synthetic scenarios before those inputs exist:

| Stage | Useful AI-assisted output | Information or decision needed before real use |
|---|---|---|
| Understand the offer | Compare brochures, extract inclusions, identify conflicting dates, draft questions | Confirm sellable facts and the actual differentiators. |
| Find schools and partners | Source-backed organization research and current adult-role candidates | Verify fit, relationship ownership, and permitted use/contact. |
| Prepare conversations | Account brief, questions, and follow-up drafts from approved notes | Conduct the relationship, approve messages, and correct interpretation. |
| Develop proposals | Assemble approved templates and flag missing trip inputs | Design the trip, approve rates and margins, confirm suppliers and validity. |
| Track bookings | Summarize approvals, outstanding contracts, and deposits with references | Confirm approval, contract, payment, and booking status. |
| Prepare delivery | Deadline lists, document checks, and handoff summaries | Manage exceptions, participants, suppliers, and live event delivery. |
| Learn afterward | Summarize approved feedback and identify follow-up opportunities | Decide service improvements and future relationship strategy. |

Prefer explicit records and calculations for prices, dates, and totals. AI can help interpret and explain those records; its prose is not a financial ledger or proof that a payment arrived. Begin with the minimum information needed for adult business relationships and aggregate group planning.

## 7. Productivity predictions and how to test them

| Area | Expectation | Confidence and limits |
|---|---|---|
| Document assembly, formatting, and repeat summaries | Likely earliest time savings once inputs and templates are stable | Relatively high confidence in direction; no measured Troen baseline yet. |
| Prospect research and conversation preparation | Useful reduction in repetitive work, with manual checks remaining | Moderate confidence; source access and ambiguous identities limit automation. |
| Follow-up organization and missing-information checks | Better visibility and consistency if people maintain records | Moderate confidence; depends on adoption and correct source data. |
| Replies, approvals, and bookings | Better preparation may help, but no reliable uplift estimate yet | Low confidence in a numerical prediction; budgets, timing, relationships, and approvals dominate. |

The **20–40% reduction in hands-on time on selected recurring preparation tasks is an untested planning hypothesis**. It is not measured savings, a whole-business forecast, a model benchmark, or a sales guarantee. Initial setup and review may produce little or no net saving.

Initially use representative school profiles, proposal revisions, and summaries to record developer preparation/review effort. Do not assign Troen a time study to receive the POC. A later savings claim needs comparable current-process and assisted-process measurements, including factual review, correction, and rework. Compare the quality and usefulness of the outputs, not just completion speed. Keep setup effort separate from recurring effort, and count subscriptions and ongoing maintenance before claiming financial savings.

For illustration only: if a recurring preparation task currently takes eight hours, a 25% reduction would release two hours before any additional maintenance. Those figures are hypothetical; they are not Troen's observed workload.

Track qualified opportunities, substantive conversations, proposal requests, approval blockers, and contract/deposit progress separately from time savings. More scraped rows or generated pages can increase review work without improving the business outcome.

## 8. A measured path to future opportunities

First prove usefulness for one event. Then test whether the same research standards, document templates, and handoffs work for another Troen event. Record the pieces that repeat and the exceptions that require specialist attention.

A plausible later offer is a repeatable research and operations-support service for performance travel. It might include periodic market research, maintained opportunity records, approved customer materials, and a small number of dependable automations. Other providers or clients are future possibilities, not an established market or a committed product launch.

Expansion should follow evidence: the Troens use the outputs, review effort is manageable, recurring benefits exceed upkeep, and ownership/access are clear. Check client confidentiality and reuse rights before reusing materials for another organization. Do not treat internal Troen records as a general-purpose prospect database or marketing portfolio.

The lasting value would be business understanding, reliable evidence, relationships, and repeatable workflows. Frontier models can improve the work as they evolve; the offer should remain useful when a model name or vendor changes.
