## 2. The model

For `N` paying travellers at `k` people to a room:

```
  price(N, k) =   consumed per person              hotel + attractions + dinners
                + commission per person            the operator's own margin
                - vendor-earned comps / N          a supplier's own ratio: REDUCES cost
                + trip-granted comps x consumed / N   free places the payers carry: INCREASES cost
                + per-group fees / N               a flat guide fee does not scale
                + coach charter total / N          DERIVED — the quote line is illegible
                + unreconciled per person          DERIVED — the residual the pages do not explain
                + 3 nights x room rate x (1/k - 1/4)   DERIVED — the room differential is unpublished
```

Everything above the two derived lines comes from the record. The base occupancy is **Quad** (4 to a room); every other occupancy is the base plus a room differential, which is why the differential and the room rate are the same question.

## 4. The hinge: two kinds of comp, moving in opposite directions

`schema.cjs` refuses to treat these as one number. **Vendor-earned comp** — *reduces_cost*. **Trip-granted comp** — *increases_cost_per_paying_traveller*. On the printed proposal both appear as bare pen numbers with nothing to say which way they push.

### 4a. Vendor-earned — reduces cost

| Payers | Supplier | Ratio worked out | Worth to the group | Effect per paying traveller |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 80 paying | Rock City Gardens | 80 ÷ 10 = 8 earned + 1 (bus driver comp) = 9 free | 9 × $19.00 = $171.00 | $171.00 ÷ 80 = $2.14 off every paying traveller |
| 90 paying | Rock City Gardens | 90 ÷ 10 = 9 earned + 1 (bus driver comp) = 10 free | 10 × $19.00 = $190.00 | $190.00 ÷ 90 = $2.11 off every paying traveller |

At 80 paying travellers that is $171.00 the operator does not have to spend, or $2.14 per paying traveller. **Where it lands is the open question.** It belongs inside the $183.00 blended attractions line, and the blend cannot be decomposed, so nothing on any page says whether that line is already net of the comps. This model treats it as gross and applies the credit. If the blend is already net, the credit is being taken twice and every price below is $2.14 too low at 80 payers.

### 4b. Trip-granted — increases cost per paying traveller

| Payers | What the free places consume | Effect per paying traveller |
|---|---|---|
| 80 paying | 3 free places × $398.16 consumed each = $1,194.48 | $1,194.48 ÷ 80 = $14.93 added to every paying traveller |
| 90 paying | 3 free places × $398.16 consumed each = $1,194.48 | $1,194.48 ÷ 90 = $13.27 added to every paying traveller |
| 100 paying | 3 free places × $398.16 consumed each = $1,194.48 | $1,194.48 ÷ 100 = $11.94 added to every paying traveller |

A comp place consumes hotel $125.16 + attractions $183.00 + dinners $90.00 = $398.16. It does not consume commission — the operator does not pay itself a margin on a place it gave away — and it does not add a coach cost, because the coach is chartered whether or not the three extra seats are filled. If the comps were instead loaded with commission, every price would rise by a further $1.31 at 80 payers.

That is consistent with the quote system printing the coach as a *per-person* line: a per-group charter divided by a headcount is exactly what a per-person coach cell is, and dividing it is what makes every headcount change a re-quote. **This is derived, not sourced.** It is a falsifiable prediction — the illegible cell should read about $274.68 at 80 payers — and it is not a claim that the charter costs this. The $113.74 per person that remains is not explained by any line on any page. It is carried openly here rather than smoothed into another figure.

### 5c. The room differential, and a reconciliation that fails

A 3-night room at rate R costs `3 × R / k` per person at k to a room, so the supplement over Quad is `3 × R × (1/k - 1/4)`. That predicts the supplements should stand in the ratio 1 : 3 : 9 regardless of headcount. They do:

| Occupancy | Published supplement over Quad | Implied room rate per room per night |
|---|---|---|
| Triple (3 to a room) | 80: $52, 90: $52, 100: $52 → mean $52.00 | $208.00 |
| Double (2 to a room) | 80: $157, 90: $156, 100: $156 → mean $156.33 | $208.44 |
| Single (1 to a room) | 80: $470, 90: $469, 100: $469 → mean $469.33 | $208.59 |

Three independent estimates agreeing within $0.59 is not a coincidence: the printed table really is built on a room rate. Pooled across all 9 supplements, the implied rate is **$208.57 per room per night — DERIVED**.

And now the part worth taking to the office. At 4 to a room that rate is $156.43 per person for 3 nights. The quote system's hotel line says **$125.16** — a gap of $31.27 per person, or $2,501.49 across 80 travellers. The two cannot be reconciled at any whole number of people to a room: $125.16 at the derived rate would need 5.00 people in each room. Either the cost line and the differential were built from different room rates, or one of them is stale. Nothing on any page says which.

## 11. Method

- **Rounding.** Every figure is rounded half up, away from zero. Working figures keep two decimal places; a price quoted to a client is rounded to the whole USD at the last step only, never at an intermediate one. The unrounded table in section 6 is printed so the rounding can be checked by hand.
- **Fitting.** Two unknowns are solved from the two extreme tiers (80 and 100); the middle tier is never used as an input, which is what makes it a check. The room rate is a single parameter fitted through the origin across all 9 published supplements at once.
- **Determinism.** This document is generated from `pipeline/trip/trips/west-henderson-chattanooga-2027-04-08-v2.trip.json` and `pipeline/trip/schema.cjs` only. No clock, no randomness, no network: `node pipeline/trip/cli.cjs check --all` compares bytes and would fail otherwise.
- **Privacy.** Business names are kept; every individual and every direct number stays a role.
